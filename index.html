US-China tariff escalation

If the United States and China escalate tariffs against each other, what happens to real incomes - including Singapore's?

A bilateral tariff wedge on both directions of US-China goods trade raises the delivered price of each partner's goods in the other's market, diverts demand towards third suppliers and moves the terms of trade; Singapore is never shocked and responds only through re-routing and price effects.

Shock: percentage points added to the applied bilateral ad-valorem tariff, floored at zero, entering the kernel through its own grammar dhat = (1 + tau_new) / (1 + tau_bar); revenue is rebated per the kernel's taurevshare closure

What this scenario does not do (4)
  • Static and one-period: there is no capital accumulation, no entry or exit and no adjustment path, so the number is a comparison of two equilibria, not a forecast of a transition.
  • Tariff revenue is rebated to households under the kernel's own closure (taurevshare); a different recycling rule would move the welfare number.
  • The calibration is the package's shipped data_tiva25_tar.mat, consumed rather than rebuilt; the manifest records this as the reason the replication status stops short of exact.
  • This is not one of the paper's printed counterfactuals, so it inherits none of the Table 1 / Table 2 verification status; only the shipped high-income versus non-high-income tariff war carries that.
Disclosures this card must show (3)

Load-bearing implementation quirks reproduced deliberately, not cleaned: convergence tested only every 20 iterations; asymmetric damping rho1 = rho*0.5; mixed-vintage output block (pre-update CPIhat/What, post-update Xhat); ROW tested as high != 1.

US applied tariffs at current levels

What does the current US partner-tariff regime imply for Singapore and its closest Southeast Asia manufacturing competitors?

−0.13% change in real income · per-capita, model counterfactual basisLAB EXTENSION
moduleWorld Bank practitioner tariff model (AO_Extended)
Mechanism, limitations & provenance
TRADE · runs natively
enginestructural adaptershocktariff · 4.20 partner-specific coverage-adjusted percentage-point increments; 4.2 is Singapore's incrementoutcome groupwelfare.real_income.static

The United States raises goods tariffs on every mapped investigated partner at once. Partner-specific delivered-price wedges shift US sourcing and world relative prices; all economies then respond through the Track A general-equilibrium trade and income channels.

Shock: US-only additional tariffs by partner, using Column B coverage-adjusted all-goods-uniform equivalents from research/scenarios/US_TARIFF_REGIME_2026-08-10.md, applied to the model's calibration-year baseline; all other model economies receive 0pp

What this scenario does not do (4)
  • Coverage stylisation: the statutory rate touches about 27% of import value; only Singapore and Mexico have published partner coverage, so uniform all-goods equivalents can misallocate sectoral substitution even when the aggregate is right.
  • Excluded sectorals: roughly two-thirds of the actual tariff increase comes from Section 232 measures, which are excluded by construction and never stacked onto this scenario.
  • Volatility: litigation, pending investigations and scheduled changes make this a high-volatility as-of snapshot, not a stable policy path.
  • Structural: the 2.5 percentage-point cross-partner dispersion means this is predominantly a US-versus-world level shock, not a rich trade-diversion experiment.
Disclosures this card must show (3)

US applied tariffs at current levels, all partners — as of 10 August 2026.

VERY HIGH. (1) Litigation: two live CIT challenges to THIS action (Liberty Justice Center class action, 24 Jul; 25 state AGs, 3 Aug); predecessors were vacated within 12 and 3 months. (2) Pending: Section 301 excess-capacity investigation of 16 economies INCLUDING SINGAPORE (initiated 11 Mar 2026, undetermined); Vietnam IP 301; China Phase One review due October. (3) Scheduled: Canada S.338 50% on ~$16bn eff. 19 Aug 2026; pharma tranche 2 on 29 Sep; furniture 1 Jan 2027. Budget Lab projects 11.1% → 11.8% by end-2026 on current law.

If US final demand for Chinese electronics falls 10 per cent, how exposed is Singapore?

−US$264mUSD million · gross-output exposure, ICIO 2022 current-price accounting basisLAB EXTENSIONINDEPENDENT REIMPLEMENTATION
moduleGlobal exposure and vulnerability to trade shocks (World Bank PRWP 11243)
Mechanism, limitations & provenance

engineevidence lensshockforeign_final_demand · -10.00 percentoutcome groupexposure.gross_output.leontief_demand.icio_mrioCOMPARE panelEXTERNAL EXPOSURE

Removing a tenth of the US final-demand cells for Chinese electronics propagates back through the world input-output inverse; Singapore's exposure is whatever its industries supply into that chain under fixed 2022 coefficients.

Shock: percentage change applied to the addressed final-demand cells of the OECD ICIO 2025 table (all six final-demand categories), propagated as delta_x = L delta_f

What this scenario does not do (4)
  • A mechanical exposure under fixed 2022 production coefficients: no price response, no substitution, no capacity constraint, no behavioural adjustment and no equilibrium. It is an accounting incidence, not a counterfactual.
  • Only the final-demand columns move. Demand for the same goods as intermediate inputs elsewhere is held fixed, so this is a partition of final demand and not of trade.
  • No transmission-path decomposition is reported: no path metric is approved for this lens, and the face-value / hidden split that is reported uses the World Bank package's convention, which is not SingStat's Direct.
  • The table is the OECD ICIO 2025 edition, reference year 2022, in USD million. Arvis et al. (2025) report no ICIO value and contain no model, so nothing here is a replication target.

CURRENT-DATA EXTENSION — OECD ICIO 2025; not a result of Arvis et al. (2025)

mechanical exposure under fixed 2022 production coefficients

CURRENT-DATA EXTENSION. Computed with the paper's indicator definitions on OECD ICIO 2025 edition, not on Eora26 1990-2022. This is not a reproduction of Arvis et al. (2025); the paper reports no ICIO values.

Only the final-demand columns of the ICIO are shocked. Demand for these goods as intermediate inputs elsewhere is held fixed; this is a partition of final demand, not of trade.

Face value includes the initial unit and the first intermediate round (I+A); SingStat's published Direct is the initial unit only.

Transmission-path decomposition is deliberately absent: no path metric is approved for this lens yet, and an unapproved construction would be a new methodology, not a reading of the table. What is reported instead is the package's own split: face value = (I + A)Δf, hidden = (L − (I + A))Δf.

How to read the matrix — three cells a reader could misread

1. TRADE × productivity is a named mapping, not native: track_a_wb11347 runs productivity through the registered bridge track_a.productivity.va_to_gross.v1: Per World Bank country c and WB-21 sector J: g_J = sum_{i in J} VA_i * g_i / sum_{i in J} VA_i (VA-weighted value-added TFP log change); alpha_J = sum_{i in J} alpha_i * OUT_i / sum_{i in J} OUT_i (gross-output-weighted primary-factor share, alpha = (VA + TLS) / OUT); hat_gross_equiv = exp(alpha_J * g_J). The unconverted value-added loading hat_va = exp(g_J) is retained as the upper bound and is never fed to the kernel as if it were a gross-output hat. The mapping is registered as track_a.productivity.va_to_gross.v1, and requiring it is what stops the value-added-versus-gross error.
