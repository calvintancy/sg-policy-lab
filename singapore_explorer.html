How much to trust these numbers

SETTLED The mechanism. The engine passes every falsifiable test (accounting identities at 10⁻¹⁴; an independent, structurally different model agrees at rank-correlation 0.987). Trust the channels: how domestic AI, cheaper imports and competitiveness interact.
HIGH Rankings & the structural story. Validated against everything the paper prints (US 0.911 vs ~0.9; sector order exact; orderings ρ ≥ 0.9).
BANDED Singapore's level. No paper value exists to check it. Quote ranges, not points: the two defensible exposure treatments bracket 0.62–0.73 pp at A2 (the occupation-mix toggle IS that band), and the micro-gain dial (20–40%) spans 0.41–0.82 — it is the dominant driver of any headline. The re-anchored adoption build gives 0.897 pp (promotion withheld, gate G4 fails on Switzerland); the +45% spread between the two builds is itself the uncertainty estimate. Singapore's profile (no printed anchor exists): top-3 exposure, foreign-heavy gains, negative competitiveness term, mid-pack outcome — robust across every setting of the dials.
DON'T LEAN The negative B1 (foreign-only) number — the extreme-openness allocation channel is the reconstruction's weakest, unresolved part (the LUX-class anomaly; Singapore is in that class). Spillover (B2/B3) magnitudes run ~⅓ of the paper's. Nothing here is a forecast or a causal policy estimate.
Basis: every systematic miss is attributed to a named input with a proven mechanism; nothing was tuned to match the paper. Full 13-item open-gap register: OECD_AI_LIMITATIONS.md + the Gate-7 audit record in the repository.
Fidelity, method & provenance

How this page computes. Adoption S-curves and shock magnitudes are the model's own formulas, exact. General-equilibrium responses use a per-country linear surrogate total ≈ T(A2) + λ·Δs + φ·Δf fitted to 9 solver runs (A1–A3, micro bands, CHN/IND-low, B1–B3; only 8 of these for the 30 non-focal countries, which lack a B1 run) — 11 solved scenarios ship as exact anchors (the 9 fit runs plus eps005 and A2corr, which change the model rather than the shock and are excluded from the fit), where s is the country's own shock and f the foreign shock averaged with value-added weights. At exact preset settings the page snaps to the real run. Singapore: λ = 18.39, φ = −5.22 — foreign productivity alone lowers Singapore's real income at fixed own-shock (the specific-capital allocation loss behind its negative foreign-only B1 result of −0.25 pp; services-corrected −0.13 pp).

Fit quality. Leave-one-out error: Singapore ≤ 0.007 pp; worst of 77 countries 0.026 pp (Ireland); zero countries over the 0.05 pp disclosure threshold. In-sample worst residual 0.025 pp. Toggles for CHN/IND, spillovers, services and the occupation-mix variant apply per-country deltas solved at A2 and held fixed across dials — a disclosed approximation. These figures are anchor-validated only: the micro-gain dial (15–45%) and the a0 dial (1–10%) both extend beyond the fitted anchor hull (micro20/micro40 = 20–40%), so outside that band the linear surrogate is an extrapolation with no anchor-confirmed error bound. Switching on several toggles at once adds their individually-disclosed, A2-frozen deltas together, which compounds the held-fixed approximation in a way that is not separately validated.

Occupation-mix variant (ALT-EXP-LFSAGG2). The primary build applies US occupational structure to every country (the paper's European Union Labour Force Survey microdata is application-gated). The variant rebuilds 73/76 countries' exposure from their own occupation-by-industry data in five tiers: Eurostat lfsa_eisn2 (32), ILOSTAT EMP_TEMP_ECO_OCU_NB_A (30, Singapore included), a 2023-vintage table for Indonesia, a bridged industry-classification table for South Africa, and national tables for CAN/HKG/TWN (incl. a verified NOC→ISCO chain). China is verified unreachable from public data — its census contains no occupation×industry cross-tab (455 tables parsed) — and stays on US structure with 8 others rather than being imputed. Validation vs the paper's digitised country-exposure figure: full-sample rank correlation 0.80 → 0.92; ZAF's exposure error 63% closed; its A2 overshoot 31% closed, IDN's 46%. Recorded as a variant, not promoted (adoption remains the dominant gap; levels improve less than rankings). Singapore: A2 0.617 → 0.730 pp (+18%) on the frozen primary build, mostly via the competitiveness channel. See research/oecd_ai/exposure_lfsagg2_notes.md.

Re-anchored adoption variant (ALT-ADOPT-REANCHOR, Gate-8 primary-designate). The primary build's adoption Step-I/II leans on Eurostat/BTOS/CSBC survey values taken largely as published, with WDI predictor proxies. The variant rebuilds Step-I/II on recovered 2024 published data instead — OECD AI Papers No. 41 Table B.4's own harmonised column, the true StatCan/INSEE/CBS survey levels, OECD-MSTI BERD and OECD-EaG predictors in place of WDI proxies, and refitted Step-II slopes — taking the printed anchor battery from 12/14 to 14/14 hits. The pre-registered Stage-2 promotion gates (config/stage2_promotion_gates.yml) were evaluated 2026-08-05: 4 of 5 PASS (B1 OECD median 0.0291 vs ~0.03; Fig-14 foreign channel 46/46; zero new B1 sign flips; IRL B1 0.157 vs 0.14), G4 FAILS — the re-anchored A2 OECD maximum (Switzerland, 0.963 pp) sits 1.37% over the paper's printed Table-2 ceiling (0.95 pp). Per the pre-registered rule, promotion is withheld: the variant remains primary-designate — a labelled, preferred variant — not primary-of-record; the frozen primary above stays the classification of record. The two builds are a genuine duality, never blended: Singapore's A2 per-capita real income is 0.617 pp on the frozen primary (0.617–0.730 pp across its exposure variants) and 0.897 pp under the re-anchored adoption build — the ≈45% spread between the two is itself the honest uncertainty estimate, since no OECD-published Singapore value exists to adjudicate between them. Always name the build when quoting a Singapore level. See research/oecd_ai/stage2_promotion_notes.md and OECD_AI_LIMITATIONS.md (reader's confidence guide, tier 3).

What this page reconstructs, and what it cannot check

What was replicated, and how. The OECD published no code and no data for this paper, so the model was rebuilt from scratch off its Annex A — exposure, adoption, the shock construction and the general-equilibrium solver — and then validated against every figure and table the paper prints. Classification: structural reconstruction, with a scoped quantitative claim for the OECD-core headline results (US A2 0.911 pp vs the paper's ~0.9; B1 median 0.0244 vs ~0.03).

Why Singapore is an estimate and not a replication. The paper prints no Singapore-specific value, so nothing on this page can be checked against it. Every Singapore number here is this repository's reconstruction, and the two defensible builds bracket it at 0.617 pp (frozen primary) to 0.897 pp (re-anchored adoption) — that spread is the honest uncertainty. Always name the build when quoting a level.
